Point tools bought à la carte (CV parsing, scheduling, outreach) can start under NZD 500 per month each but rarely talk to each other. ## What "AI automation" means for a recruitment agency

AI automation is broader than any single tool. For a recruitment desk it usually spans five workflows, and the cost depends on how many you take on:

- **Candidate sourcing and matching:** ranking applicants against a brief, surfacing passive candidates from your existing database.
- **CV parsing and screening:** turning unstructured CVs into structured records and shortlists.
- **Voice screening and inbound call handling:** answering and qualifying candidates by phone, covered in depth in our breakdown of [how much an AI voice agent costs for recruitment agencies](how-much-does-an-ai-voice-agent-cost-for-recruitment-agencies.md).
- **Interview scheduling and coordination:** booking across candidate and consultant calendars without the back-and-forth.
- **ATS data entry and outreach:** writing structured outcomes into your system and running follow-up sequences.

A managed programme usually starts with one or two of these and adds more once the numbers hold. That staging is the single biggest lever on cost.

## The three cost tiers in New Zealand dollars

### 1. Point tools you assemble yourself (from ~NZD 500 per workflow, plus your time)

Standalone tools for CV parsing, scheduling or outreach sequencing can each start under NZD 500 per month. On paper this is the cheapest route. In practice the tools do not share data: a parser that does not write into JobAdder or Bullhorn produces shortlists your consultants retype by hand. Agencies that succeed here usually have an in-house technical person who owns the integrations. Most do not, and end up moving to a managed tier once the manual glue work outweighs the licence saving.

### 2. Managed automation programme (NZD 5,000 to NZD 25,000 setup, NZD 1,000 to NZD 6,000 per month)

This is the tier most New Zealand agencies actually buy. A partner maps your workflows, builds the automations, connects them to your ATS and calendar stack, and owns the monitoring and iteration. The monthly fee covers hosting, model usage, and ongoing tuning as real candidate and client data flows through.

Within the range, price moves on three things: the number of workflows automated, call and application volume, and integration depth. Automating one workflow that logs a summary into a shared inbox is cheap. Automating three workflows that create structured candidate records, update application stages, and book interviews against consultants' live calendars is not.

### 3. Custom enterprise builds (NZD 25,000 to NZD 75,000+ setup)

Larger agencies and RPO providers with compliance requirements, multiple brands, or bespoke ATS setups sit here. The premium pays for security review, audit trails, data residency arrangements, load handling for campaign spikes, and formal testing before anything touches a live candidate or client. At this tier automation is treated as operational infrastructure with an owner, which is the correct framing for any agency running thousands of candidate conversations a month.

## The cost drivers quotes leave out

**Integration depth.** The single biggest variable. Automation that cannot write into your ATS produces outputs nobody uses. Ask any vendor exactly which fields it will create or update in your system, and what happens when the ATS API is down mid-process.

**Compliance and candidate data.** In New Zealand the Privacy Act 2020 applies to candidate data handled by an AI workflow exactly as it does to a consultant's notes. Right-to-work checks, data retention and candidate consent must be designed in from day one. Retrofitting them after a complaint is where budgets blow out.

**Data quality in your ATS.** AI matching is only as good as the records it reads. Agencies with years of inconsistent tagging often need a data clean-up before matching automation earns its keep, and that clean-up is real work that rarely appears in the headline quote.

**Change management.** The tool is the cheap part. Consultants have to trust the shortlist and actually use the booked interviews. Budget for the fortnight of supervised use where the automation earns that trust, or the licence becomes shelfware.

## What a sensible buying process looks like

Once you know the budget, the harder question is where to start. Pick the one workflow costing your consultants the most hours, usually CV screening or interview scheduling, and run it as a paid pilot with clear success measures: time saved per role, shortlist quality, and consultant hours returned to client-facing work. A four-to-six week single-workflow pilot at the managed tier typically lands between NZD 3,000 and NZD 8,000 all-in, which is less than one month of a resourcer's fully loaded cost. Agencies that treat the first automation as an operational system with an owner, monitoring and an improvement loop, rather than a set-and-forget tool, are the ones still using it a year later.

## FAQ

### How much does AI automation cost for a small recruitment agency in New Zealand?

For a boutique agency automating a single workflow, expect NZD 3,000 to NZD 8,000 for a six-week managed pilot, then NZD 1,000 to NZD 2,500 per month to run it. Point tools alone can be cheaper monthly but shift the integration and upkeep onto your team.

### What recruitment processes are worth automating first?

Start with the workflow eating the most consultant hours with the clearest success measure. For most agencies that is CV screening or interview scheduling, because both are high-volume, repetitive, and easy to measure. Voice screening and outbound sequencing usually come second once the first workflow proves out.

### How are AI agents transforming recruitment processes?

The practical change is coverage and consistency rather than replacement. Automations screen every application against the same criteria, book interviews without phone tag, and write structured outcomes into the ATS immediately. Consultants spend their time on shortlists, client relationships and offers instead of administration.

### Can AI automation integrate with our existing ATS or CRM?

Usually yes, and it should be a hard requirement before you sign anything. Mainstream systems such as JobAdder, Bullhorn and Vincere can send and receive structured records through their APIs. The integration effort is what separates the pricing tiers, so ask vendors to demonstrate a write into your specific system, not "an ATS" in general.

### Is candidate data handled by AI subject to the Privacy Act 2020?

Yes. Candidate information processed by an AI workflow is personal information under the Privacy Act 2020, with the same obligations around consent, use and retention that apply to a human consultant. A competent provider designs consent capture and data handling into the workflow from the start rather than bolting them on later.
