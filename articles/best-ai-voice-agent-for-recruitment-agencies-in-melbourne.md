## What "best" means for a Melbourne agency

Voice-agent vendors compete on demos, but a demo is the easiest part. For a recruitment agency the value shows up in three unglamorous places: whether the call outcome lands in your ATS as a structured record, whether the agent answers when a candidate calls at 8pm, and whether someone is tuning it as you hear real screening calls. An agent that produces transcripts nobody reads is no better than voicemail, however polished the voice sounds.

So the best option for your agency is the one that scores highest on operational fit, not the one with the smoothest sales call. That reframing matters in Melbourne, where agency competition is dense across healthcare and aged care, construction and infrastructure (the state's long pipeline of public build work), professional services and a large public-sector and high-volume temp and contract market. Many of those roles are filled in volume and against a clock, and most strong candidates are already working, so the calls that win placements happen outside office hours.

## The five criteria to score every option against

**1. ATS write-back.** The single biggest separator. JobAdder is the dominant ATS across Australian recruitment, with Bullhorn and Vincere close behind, so ask every vendor to demonstrate a write into your exact system, creating or updating the specific fields your consultants use. "It integrates with an ATS" is not an answer. Ask what happens when the API is down mid-call.

**2. Latency and voice quality.** Cheap stacks introduce pauses that make candidates hang up. A screening call is an employer-brand touchpoint, so production-quality voice and low-latency pipelines justify a higher per-minute cost. Test this on a real mobile line, not a browser demo, because Australian mobile networks expose latency that a laptop hides.

**3. After-hours coverage.** For Melbourne recruitment this is the strongest single argument for a voice agent at all. Candidates who already have jobs call in the evening, and healthcare, aged care and shift-based desks run enquiries around the clock. An agent that answers at 8pm captures people your competitors' offices miss, and once the agent exists that coverage costs nothing extra per call.

**4. Compliance and consent.** Candidate data an AI agent collects sits under the federal Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles exactly as a consultant's notes do. Recording is where Victoria has its own rules: under the Surveillance Devices Act 1999 (Vic), a party to a private conversation may generally record it, which is a lighter test than New South Wales applies, but relying on that participant exception is not a substitute for disclosure. Build a clear recording notice and consent capture into the greeting and the flow anyway, because it is better practice and because desks that also screen interstate hit stricter regimes. If any of your candidates are screened across the border, treat all-party consent as the default.

**5. Ownership after go-live.** The best agent in month one degrades by month three if nobody tunes it. Decide before you buy whether that owner is your team (self-serve) or the partner (managed). This is the real build-versus-buy question, not the software licence.

## Build it yourself or buy it managed

Self-serve platforms such as Retell and Vapi are genuinely strong foundations. They give you the call handling, the voice models and the flow builder, and usage sits around USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute plus telephony. If you have a technical person who can own prompts, integrations and monitoring, building on one of these can be the best-value route and keeps you closest to the underlying platform.

The catch is that candidate screening is not a simple flow. It branches on availability, right-to-work status (visa and work-rights checks matter more in the Australian market), notice periods and salary expectations, and the results have to land somewhere consultants will act on them. Most Melbourne agencies do not have spare engineering capacity, which is why a managed agent (a partner builds and owns it on top of a platform like these) is the more common buy. The trade-off is cost against control. ## Melbourne is not Sydney on compliance

It is tempting to treat the whole Australian market as one rulebook, but recording law is set by each state. A Melbourne desk operating only in Victoria has more latitude to record a call it is party to than a Sydney desk does, because New South Wales does not allow single-party consent for a private conversation. If your agency runs desks in both cities, or screens candidates who pick up interstate, the safe design is the stricter one: disclose and capture consent on every call. ## FAQ

### How are AI voice agents transforming recruitment in Melbourne?

The change is coverage and consistency rather than replacement. Agents answer every inbound candidate call including evenings and weekends, run identical structured screenings so every candidate is asked the same questions, and write structured results into the ATS immediately. Consultants spend their time on shortlists and client work instead of phone tag, which matters in Melbourne's high-volume healthcare, aged care and temp desks where speed to a good candidate wins the placement.

### Why are AI voice agents so hard to customise without getting locked into vendor APIs?

Because the flexibility lives at the platform layer, and cheaper managed offerings hide it. If avoiding lock-in matters to you, favour agents built on open platforms (Retell, Vapi and similar) where you can see and edit the call logic, and ask any managed partner whether you retain access to the prompts, flows and integrations if you leave. The best arrangements keep the underlying configuration portable rather than trapped behind a proprietary wrapper.

### Can non-engineers build the call flows themselves?

Up to a point. The no-code builders on the main platforms handle straightforward branching well, but recruitment screening logic and reliable ATS write-back usually reach a wall where someone technical is needed. This is exactly the line that separates a self-serve build from a managed one, so be honest about your team's capacity before committing to run it yourself.

### Is it legal to record AI screening calls in Melbourne?

Yes, with conditions. Candidate data handling must comply with the federal Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. On recording, the Surveillance Devices Act 1999 (Vic) generally lets a party to a private conversation record it, which is lighter than the New South Wales rule, but you should still disclose recording at the start of every call and capture consent in the flow. Store candidate data the way you would any sensitive record, and treat all-party consent as the default if you also screen candidates in other states.

### Will candidates actually talk to an AI voice agent?

Completion rates on well-built screening agents are consistently strong when the agent is upfront about what it is, keeps the call under ten minutes and offers a human follow-up. Candidates tolerate AI screening the way they tolerate online application forms. What they do not tolerate is silence, and an agency answering instantly at 8pm beats one that calls back in two days.
