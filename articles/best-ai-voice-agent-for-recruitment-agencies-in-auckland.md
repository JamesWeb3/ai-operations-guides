## What "best" means for an Auckland agency

Voice-agent vendors compete on demos, but a demo is the easiest part. For a recruitment agency the value shows up in three unglamorous places: whether the call outcome lands in your ATS as a structured record, whether the agent answers when a candidate calls at 8pm, and whether someone is tuning it as you hear real screening calls. An agent that produces transcripts nobody reads is not better than voicemail, however impressive the voice sounds.

So the best option for your agency is the one that scores highest on operational fit, not the one with the smoothest sales call. That reframing matters most in Auckland, where the candidate market is tight and most applicants are currently employed, so the calls that win placements happen outside office hours.

## The five criteria to score every option against

**1. ATS write-back.** The single biggest separator. Ask every vendor to demonstrate a write into your exact system (JobAdder, Bullhorn, Vincere or your CRM), creating or updating the specific fields your consultants use. "It integrates with an ATS" is not an answer. Ask what happens when the API is down mid-call.

**2. Latency and voice quality.** Cheap stacks introduce pauses that make candidates hang up. A screening call is an employer-brand touchpoint, so production-quality voice and low-latency pipelines are worth the higher per-minute cost. Test this on a real phone line, not a browser demo, because mobile networks expose latency that a laptop hides.

**3. After-hours coverage.** For Auckland recruitment this is the strongest single argument for a voice agent at all. Candidates who already have jobs call in the evening. An agent that answers at 8pm captures people your competitors' offices miss, and once the agent exists that coverage costs nothing extra per call.

**4. Compliance and consent.** The Privacy Act 2020 applies to candidate data an AI agent collects exactly as it applies to a consultant's notes. Recording a call you are party to is lawful in New Zealand, but disclosure and sound data handling should be built into the greeting and the flow. If you screen candidates in Australia too, several states require all-party consent, so the safe default is to disclose recording at the start of every call. Agencies running a Sydney desk should read our companion guide to the [best AI voice agent for recruitment agencies in Sydney](best-ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-agencies-in-sydney.md), where the New South Wales recording rules change the compliance picture, and those with a Victorian desk should compare the [best AI voice agent for recruitment agencies in Melbourne](best-ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-agencies-in-melbourne.md), where the recording test is lighter again.

**5. Ownership after go-live.** The best agent in month one degrades by month three if nobody tunes it. Decide before you buy whether that owner is your team (self-serve) or the partner (managed). This is the real build-versus-buy question, not the software licence.

## Build it yourself or buy it managed

Self-serve platforms such as Retell and Vapi are genuinely strong foundations. They give you the call handling, the voice models and the flow builder, and usage sits around USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute plus telephony. If you have a technical person who can own prompts, integrations and monitoring, building on one of these can be the best-value route and keeps you closest to the underlying platform.

The catch is that candidate screening is not a simple flow. It branches on availability, right-to-work status, notice periods and salary expectations, and the results have to land somewhere consultants will act on them. Most Auckland agencies do not have spare engineering capacity, which is why a managed agent (a partner builds and owns it on top of a platform like these) is the more common buy. The trade-off is cost against control.

Score two or three options against the five criteria above using your own ATS, not a generic demo. ## FAQ

### How are AI voice agents transforming recruitment in Auckland?

The change is coverage and consistency rather than replacement. Agents answer every inbound candidate call including evenings and weekends, run identical structured screenings so every candidate is asked the same questions, and write structured results into the ATS immediately. Consultants spend their time on shortlists and client work instead of phone tag, which matters in a tight Auckland market where speed to a good candidate wins the placement.

### Why are AI voice agents so hard to customise without getting locked into vendor APIs?

Because the flexibility lives at the platform layer, and cheaper managed offerings hide it. If avoiding lock-in matters to you, favour agents built on open platforms (Retell, Vapi and similar) where you can see and edit the call logic, and ask any managed partner whether you retain access to the prompts, flows and integrations if you leave. The best arrangements keep the underlying configuration portable rather than trapped behind a proprietary wrapper.

### Can non-engineers build the call flows themselves?

Up to a point. The no-code builders on the main platforms handle straightforward branching well, but recruitment screening logic and reliable ATS write-back usually reach a wall where someone technical is needed. This is exactly the line that separates a self-serve build from a managed one, so be honest about your team's capacity before committing to run it yourself.

### Will candidates actually talk to an AI voice agent?

Completion rates on well-built screening agents are consistently strong when the agent is upfront about what it is, keeps the call under ten minutes and offers a human follow-up. Candidates tolerate AI screening the way they tolerate online application forms. What they do not tolerate is silence, and an agency answering instantly at 8pm beats one that calls back in two days.

### Is it legal to record AI screening calls in New Zealand?

Yes, with conditions. Recording a call you are party to is lawful in New Zealand, but candidate data handling must comply with the Privacy Act 2020. Disclose recording at the start of every call and store candidate data the way you would any sensitive record. If you also screen candidates in Australia, treat all-party consent as the default because several states require it.
