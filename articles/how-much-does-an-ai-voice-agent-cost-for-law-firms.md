# How Much Does an AI Voice Agent Cost for Law Firms?

> For a law firm in New Zealand or Australia, an AI voice agent typically costs NZD 600 to NZD 3,000 per month for a managed service, on top of a one-off build of NZD 3,000 to NZD 12,000. Self-serve platforms look cheaper (roughly USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute) but push the confidentiality, conflict-checking and matter-integration work onto your own team. What moves the price is not the AI: it is client confidentiality, conflict-of-interest screening, and how deeply the agent plugs into your practice management system. Demand is early: across Sentry AI's own Google Search Console data for the 90 days to August 2026, the AI voice agent query cluster drew more than 690 impressions against our site, yet not one of those searches named a law firm or legal practice, so legal has no incumbent answer at all.

The short answer: budget NZD 600 to NZD 3,000 per month for a managed AI voice agent, plus a setup fee of NZD 3,000 to NZD 12,000 depending on how tightly it needs to integrate with your practice management and phone systems. A custom build with full confidentiality and conflict-check sign-off starts around NZD 18,000 up front. If you are technical and prepared to run it in-house, platform usage fees alone can sit under NZD 900 per month at moderate call volumes, before you count the time to build and maintain it.

The rest of this guide breaks down where those numbers come from, which tier suits which kind of firm, and the legal-specific cost drivers that quotes tend to leave out.

## The three pricing tiers

### 1. Self-serve platforms (from ~NZD 250 per month, plus your time)

Platforms in this tier let you assemble a voice agent yourself: you write the prompts, connect a phone number, and pay per minute of talk time, currently around USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per minute plus telephony charges. For a firm taking 400 five-minute enquiry calls per month, that is 2,000 minutes, or roughly NZD 250 to NZD 950 in usage before the build.

The build is the catch. A legal intake call is not a simple FAQ: it needs to identify the practice area (family, conveyancing, litigation, wills and estates), capture enough detail to run a conflict check, avoid giving anything that could be construed as legal advice, and write the result somewhere a fee earner will actually see it. The comparison with recruitment is instructive, and our breakdown of [how much an AI voice agent costs for recruitment agencies](how-much-does-an-ai-voice-agent-cost-for-recruitment-agencies.md) shows the same pattern: firms that start self-serve usually either have an in-house technical owner or migrate to a managed tier within a few months.

### 2. Managed voice agent from an AI partner (NZD 3,000 to NZD 12,000 setup, NZD 600 to NZD 3,000 per month)

This is the tier most firms in New Zealand and Australia actually buy. A partner designs the intake flows with you, builds the agent, connects it to your practice management system (LEAP, Actionstep, Smokeball and similar), and owns the monitoring and iteration. The monthly fee covers hosting, platform usage, and ongoing tuning as you hear real enquiry calls and adjust.

Within the range, three things move the price: call volume, the number of distinct call types (new-client enquiries, existing-matter updates, appointment booking, payment and trust queries), and integration depth. An agent that emails a call summary to reception is cheap. An agent that runs a preliminary conflict check, opens a prospective-matter record, and books an initial consultation against the right practitioner's calendar is not.

### 3. Custom enterprise builds (NZD 18,000 to NZD 60,000+ setup)

Larger firms, multi-office practices and those with strict information-security obligations sit here. The premium pays for security review, audit trails, data residency arrangements, load handling for enquiry spikes (a media mention or a class-action sign-up can flood the phones), and formal testing before anything speaks to a member of the public. At this tier the voice agent is treated as operational infrastructure rather than a tool, which is the right framing for any firm fielding thousands of enquiries a year.

## The cost drivers quotes leave out

**Confidentiality and privilege.** A law firm's duty of confidence is stricter than a general business privacy obligation. In New Zealand, client information is protected under the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006 conduct rules as well as the Privacy Act 2020. Anything a caller discloses to an intake agent may attract legal professional privilege, so the agent's transcripts, storage and access controls must be designed to protect it. Building this in from day one is straightforward: retrofitting it after an information-handling complaint is not.

**Conflict checking.** The single most legal-specific variable. An intake agent that captures the other side's name and the matter type can run a preliminary conflict screen against your existing clients before a fee earner ever picks up. That is genuinely valuable, and it is also the integration most likely to lift you from the self-serve tier into the managed one, because it requires a reliable read against your practice management database.

**Avoiding inadvertent legal advice.** An agent must gather facts without answering the legal question. A poorly scoped agent that says something a caller treats as advice is a professional-conduct risk, not just a bad customer experience. Scoping and testing this boundary is real work, and it is part of what the managed and enterprise tiers are paying for.

**After-hours coverage.** One of the strongest cost-benefit cases for legal specifically. Distressed callers (a family matter, an arrest, an urgent conveyancing deadline) ring outside business hours, and the firm that answers at 9pm captures the client the firm that returns the call in two days loses. Once the agent exists, this coverage comes at no marginal cost per call.

## FAQ

### Will an AI voice agent give clients legal advice by mistake?

Not if it is scoped correctly, and this is a hard requirement before you deploy. A competent intake agent gathers facts (party names, matter type, urgency, contact details) and explicitly declines to answer the legal question, routing the caller to a practitioner instead. Testing this boundary thoroughly is part of what separates a managed build from a self-serve experiment.

### Can it run a conflict check before a lawyer takes the call?

Yes, where it integrates with your practice management system. The agent captures the relevant names and matter details and screens them against your existing client and matter records, flagging a possible conflict for human review. This is one of the strongest reasons legal firms move to the managed tier rather than a self-serve platform.

### Is it legal to record intake calls in New Zealand and Australia?

Yes, with conditions. In New Zealand, recording a call you are party to is lawful, but client information must be handled under the Privacy Act 2020 and the profession's confidentiality obligations. In Australia, consent rules vary by state and several require all-party consent, the same consideration that shapes vendor choice for any firm weighing the [best AI voice agent for recruitment agencies in Sydney](best-ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-agencies-in-sydney.md). The safe default is to disclose recording at the start of every call, which a competent provider builds into the greeting.

### How does an AI voice agent handle client confidentiality?

Through access controls, encryption, and storage designed for privileged information from the outset. Ask any vendor exactly where transcripts are stored, who can access them, what data residency applies, and how the agent's records flow into your matter files. If a vendor cannot answer those questions precisely, they have not built for legal.

### How long does implementation take?

A single managed call type is typically live in three to five weeks: one week of flow design and integration scoping, one to two weeks of build and internal testing, then a supervised go-live with a fee earner reviewing every call. Custom enterprise builds run eight weeks or more once security review and conflict-check integration are included.
