Self-serve platforms are cheaper on paper (roughly USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute) but shift the build and maintenance burden onto your team. Deep ATS integration, compliance requirements and call volume are what move the price, not the AI itself. The short answer: budget NZD 500 to NZD 2,500 per month for a managed AI voice agent, plus a setup fee of NZD 2,000 to NZD 10,000 depending on how deeply it needs to plug into your ATS and phone stack. If you want a custom enterprise build with full compliance sign-off, expect NZD 15,000 or more up front. If you are technical and happy to run it yourself, platform usage fees alone can come in under NZD 800 per month at moderate call volumes.

The rest of this guide breaks down where those numbers come from, which tier fits which kind of agency, and the cost drivers that quotes often leave out.

## The three pricing tiers

### 1. Self-serve platforms (from ~NZD 200 per month, plus your time)

Platforms in this tier let you assemble a voice agent yourself: you configure the prompts, connect a phone number, and pay per minute of talk time. Usage pricing across the main platforms currently sits around USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute, plus telephony charges.

For a recruitment agency running, say, 500 five-minute screening calls per month, that is 2,500 minutes: roughly NZD 300 to NZD 1,200 in usage before you count the build. The catch is that "the build" is not a weekend job. Candidate screening flows have branching logic (availability, right-to-work status, notice periods, salary expectations), and the agent has to write results somewhere your consultants will actually look. Most agencies that start here either have an in-house technical person or end up moving to a managed tier.

### 2. Managed voice agent from an AI partner (NZD 2,000 to NZD 10,000 setup, NZD 500 to NZD 2,500 per month)

This is the tier most agencies in New Zealand and Australia actually buy. A partner designs the call flows with you, builds the agent, connects it to your ATS or CRM, and owns the monitoring and iteration. The monthly fee covers hosting, platform usage, and ongoing tuning as you hear real candidate calls and adjust.

Within the range, the price moves on three things: call volume, the number of distinct call types (inbound candidate enquiries, outbound screening, interview scheduling, reference checking), and integration depth. An agent that logs a call summary into a shared inbox is cheap. An agent that creates structured candidate records, updates application stages, and books interviews against consultants' calendars is not.

### 3. Custom enterprise builds (NZD 15,000 to NZD 50,000+ setup)

Larger agencies and RPO providers with compliance requirements, multiple brands, or bespoke ATS setups sit here. The premium pays for security review, audit trails, custom data residency arrangements, load handling for campaign spikes, and formal testing before anything touches a live candidate. At this tier the voice agent is treated as operational infrastructure rather than a tool, which is the correct framing for any agency running thousands of candidate conversations per month.

## The cost drivers quotes leave out

**Compliance and recording.** In New Zealand, the Privacy Act 2020 applies to candidate data collected by an AI agent exactly as it does to a human consultant's notes. In Australia, the Privacy Act 1988 and state-level call recording rules apply, and several states require all-party consent to record (New South Wales among them, which shapes the choice for any agency weighing the [best AI voice agent for recruitment agencies in Sydney](best-ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-agencies-in-sydney.md)). Building consent capture and data handling into the flow is straightforward if it is designed in from day one, and expensive if it is retrofitted after a complaint.

**Integration depth.** The single biggest variable. A voice agent that cannot write into your ATS produces transcripts nobody reads. Ask any vendor exactly which fields it will create or update in your system, and what happens when the ATS API is down mid-call.

**After-hours coverage.** One of the strongest cost-benefit cases for recruitment specifically. Candidates apply and call outside business hours because most of them are currently employed. An AI agent answering at 8pm is capturing candidates your competitors' offices are missing, and this coverage comes at no marginal cost once the agent exists.

**Latency and voice quality.** Cheap stacks introduce awkward pauses that cause candidates to hang up. Production-quality voice models and low-latency pipelines cost more per minute and are worth it for any call a candidate might judge you on. A screening call is an employer-brand touchpoint.

## FAQ

### How are AI voice agents transforming recruitment processes?

The practical change is coverage and consistency rather than replacement. Agents answer every inbound candidate call including evenings and weekends, run identical structured screenings so every candidate gets asked the same questions, and write structured results into the ATS immediately. Consultants spend their time on shortlists and client work instead of phone tag.

### Can an AI voice agent integrate with our existing ATS or CRM?

Usually yes, and this should be a hard requirement before you sign anything. Mainstream systems (JobAdder, Bullhorn, Vincere, and most CRMs with an API) can receive call outcomes as structured records. The integration effort is what separates the pricing tiers, so ask vendors to demonstrate a write into your specific system, not "an ATS" in general.

### Will candidates actually talk to an AI?

Completion rates on well-built screening agents are consistently strong when the agent is upfront about what it is, keeps the call under ten minutes, and offers a human follow-up. Candidates tolerate AI screening the way they tolerate online application forms: what they do not tolerate is silence, and an agency that answers instantly at 8pm beats one that calls back in two days.

### Is it legal to record AI screening calls in New Zealand and Australia?

Yes, with conditions. In New Zealand, recording a call you are party to is lawful, but candidate data handling must comply with the Privacy Act 2020. In Australia, consent rules vary by state and several require all-party consent, so agents operating across both markets should simply disclose recording at the start of every call. A competent provider builds this into the greeting.

### How long does implementation take?

A single managed call type is typically live in two to four weeks: one week of flow design and integration, one to two weeks of build and internal testing, then a supervised go-live. Custom enterprise builds run eight weeks or more once security review and staged rollout are included.
