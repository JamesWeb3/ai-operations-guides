## What "best" means for a national agency

Voice-agent vendors compete on demos, but the demo is the easy part. For a recruitment agency the value lands in three unglamorous places: whether the call outcome arrives in your ATS as a structured record, whether the agent answers when a candidate calls at 8pm, and whether someone is tuning it as real screening calls come in. An agent that produces transcripts nobody reads is not better than voicemail, however good the voice sounds.

For an agency with more than one branch, a fourth question matters: can one agent serve every desk without a separate build per city? The best arrangement runs a single agent that routes by role, region or phone number, so a candidate calling a Christchurch construction desk and one calling a Wellington policy desk both get a consistent, structured screening, and both outcomes land in the right consultant's queue. That consistency is the point. A national agency's brand is only as even as its worst-staffed branch on its busiest day, and an agent screens identically whether it is fielding one call or forty.

## The five criteria to score every option against

**1. ATS write-back.** The single biggest separator. Ask every vendor to demonstrate a write into your exact system (JobAdder, Bullhorn, Vincere or your CRM), creating or updating the specific fields your consultants actually use. "It integrates with an ATS" is not an answer. Ask what happens when the API is down mid-call.

**2. Latency and voice quality.** Cheap stacks introduce pauses that make candidates hang up. A screening call is an employer-brand touchpoint, so production-quality voice and low-latency pipelines earn their higher per-minute cost. Test on a real phone line, not a browser demo, because mobile and rural networks expose latency a laptop hides, and a national agency fields plenty of calls from outside the main centres.

**3. After-hours coverage.** For New Zealand recruitment this is the strongest single argument for a voice agent at all. Candidates who already have jobs call in the evening, and a national desk spans working patterns from shift workers in the regions to office staff in the cities. An agent that answers at 8pm captures people your competitors' offices miss, and once it exists that coverage costs nothing extra per call, whether the candidate is in Invercargill or Auckland.

**4. Compliance and consent.** The Privacy Act 2020 applies to candidate data an AI agent collects exactly as it applies to a consultant's notes. Recording a call you are party to is lawful in New Zealand, but disclosure and sound data handling belong in the greeting and the flow, not bolted on later. If your agency also runs an Australian desk, several states require all-party consent, so the safe default is to disclose recording at the start of every call. Agencies with a trans-Tasman presence should read our companion guide to the [best AI voice agent for recruitment agencies in Sydney](best-ai-voice-agent-for-recruitment-agencies-in-sydney.md), where the New South Wales recording rules shift the compliance picture.

**5. Ownership after go-live.** The best agent in month one degrades by month three if nobody tunes it. Decide before you buy whether that owner is your team (self-serve) or the partner (managed). Across several branches this decision compounds: one owner tuning one shared agent is manageable, while one unowned agent per city quietly rots in parallel. This is the real build-versus-buy question, not the software licence.

## Build it yourself or buy it managed

Self-serve platforms such as Retell and Vapi are genuinely strong foundations. They give you the call handling, the voice models and the flow builder, and usage sits around USD 0.07 to USD 0.30 per call minute plus telephony. If you have a technical person who can own prompts, integrations and monitoring, building on one of these can be the best-value route and keeps you closest to the underlying platform.

The catch is that candidate screening is not a simple flow. It branches on availability, right-to-work status, notice periods and salary expectations, and the results have to land somewhere consultants will act on them. Most New Zealand agencies do not carry spare engineering capacity, which is why a managed agent (a partner builds and owns it on top of a platform like these) is the more common buy. ## FAQ

### How are AI voice agents transforming recruitment in New Zealand?

The change is coverage and consistency rather than replacement. Agents answer every inbound candidate call including evenings and weekends, run identical structured screenings so every candidate is asked the same questions regardless of which branch they called, and write structured results into the ATS immediately. Consultants spend their time on shortlists and client work instead of phone tag, which matters most for a national agency trying to keep service even across cities and regions.

### Can one AI voice agent cover multiple branches across the country?

Yes, and that is usually the better design. A single agent routed by role, region or inbound number keeps screening consistent and gives you one place to tune, rather than a separate build per city that drifts apart over time. It also concentrates your compliance and ATS logic in one flow, so a Privacy Act disclosure or a write-back fix ships to every desk at once.

### Can a non-engineer build the call flows for a recruitment agent?

Up to a point. The no-code builders on the main platforms handle straightforward branching well, but recruitment screening logic and reliable ATS write-back usually reach a wall where someone technical is needed. This is exactly the line that separates a self-serve build from a managed one, so be honest about your team's capacity before committing to run it yourself.

### Will candidates in New Zealand actually talk to an AI voice agent?

Completion rates on well-built screening agents are consistently strong when the agent is upfront about what it is, keeps the call under ten minutes and offers a human follow-up. Candidates tolerate AI screening the way they tolerate online application forms. What they do not tolerate is silence, and an agency answering instantly at 8pm beats one that calls back in two days.

### Is it legal to record AI screening calls in New Zealand?

Yes, with conditions. Recording a call you are party to is lawful in New Zealand, but candidate data handling must comply with the Privacy Act 2020. Disclose recording at the start of every call and store candidate data the way you would any sensitive record. If you also screen candidates in Australia, treat all-party consent as the default because several states require it.
